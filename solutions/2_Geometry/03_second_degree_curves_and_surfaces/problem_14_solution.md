# Exercise 14

**Problem:** Compare

$$
x^2+y^2=z^2
$$

and

$$
x^2+y^2=4.
$$

For each surface, describe the cross-sections $z=c$, $x=0$, and $y=0$, make a sketch, and explain why the radius of the cross-section depends on $z$ in the first case but is constant in the second.

---

## Relevant theorem / definition

- A **cone** is a ruled surface generated by lines passing through a fixed apex (homogeneous equation).
- A **cylinder** with rulings parallel to the $z$-axis is generated by translating a plane curve parallel to the $z$-axis (equation contains no $z$ variable).

---

## Solution

### 1. Surface $x^2+y^2=z^2$ (Circular Double Cone)

1. Cross-sections by the planes $z = c$:

$$
x^2 + y^2 = c^2
$$

   - **For $c \neq 0$:** Circle in the horizontal plane $z = c$ centered at $(0, 0, c)$ with radius $r(c) = |c|$.
   - **For $c = 0$:** $x^2 + y^2 = 0 \implies (0, 0, 0)$, a single point (the apex).

2. Cross-section by the plane $x = 0$:

$$
y^2 = z^2 \iff z^2 - y^2 = 0 \iff (z - y)(z + y) = 0 \implies z = \pm y
$$

   Two intersecting straight lines through the origin in the $yz$-plane with slopes $\pm 1$.

3. Cross-section by the plane $y = 0$:

$$
x^2 = z^2 \iff z^2 - x^2 = 0 \iff (z - x)(z + x) = 0 \implies z = \pm x
$$

   Two intersecting straight lines through the origin in the $xz$-plane with slopes $\pm 1$.

4. Sketch of the cone:

```text
               z
         \     ^     /
          \    |    /     z = c (circle, radius = |c|)
           \   |   /
            \  |  /
  -------------+-------------> x
            /  |  \       apex (0,0,0)
           /   |   \
          /    |    \
         /     |     \
```

---

### 2. Surface $x^2+y^2=4$ (Circular Cylinder)

1. Cross-sections by the planes $z = c$:

$$
x^2 + y^2 = 4 = 2^2
$$

   For **every** value of $c \in \mathbb{R}$, this is a circle of constant radius $r = 2$ in the plane $z = c$ centered at $(0, 0, c)$.

2. Cross-section by the plane $x = 0$:

$$
y^2 = 4 \implies y = \pm 2
$$

   Two parallel vertical straight lines $y = 2$ and $y = -2$ parallel to the $z$-axis in the $yz$-plane.

3. Cross-section by the plane $y = 0$:

$$
x^2 = 4 \implies x = \pm 2
$$

   Two parallel vertical straight lines $x = 2$ and $x = -2$ parallel to the $z$-axis in the $xz$-plane.

4. Sketch of the cylinder:

```text
               z
          |    ^    |
          |----+----|     z = c (circle, radius = 2)
          |    |    |
  --------+----+----+--------> x
       (-2,0)  |  (2,0)
          |    |    |
          |----+----|
          |    |    |
```

---

### 3. Why the Radius Depends on $z$ in the First Case but is Constant in the Second

- **For the cone $x^2 + y^2 = z^2$:**
  The equation is algebraically **homogeneous of degree 2**. If $(x, y, z)$ is on the surface, then for any scalar $t \in \mathbb{R}$, $(tx, ty, tz)$ also satisfies the equation:

$$
(tx)^2 + (ty)^2 = t^2(x^2 + y^2) = t^2 z^2 = (tz)^2
$$

  The surface is a collection of straight rays emanating from the origin. The distance to the $z$-axis is:

$$
r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = \sqrt{z^2} = |z|
$$

  The radius is directly proportional to $|z|$; it shrinks linearly to $0$ as $z \to 0$ and expands linearly as $|z| \to \infty$.

- **For the cylinder $x^2 + y^2 = 4$:**
  The coordinate $z$ is **entirely absent** from the equation. In $\mathbb{R}^3$, if a point $(x, y)$ satisfies $x^2 + y^2 = 4$, then the point $(x, y, z)$ satisfies the equation for **all** $z \in (-\infty, \infty)$. The surface is generated by translating the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ along the $z$-axis. Because $z$ is a free parameter, every horizontal cross-section at any height $z = c$ is an identical copy of the base circle, so the radius is constant ($r = 2$) regardless of $z$.

---

## Result

$$
x^2+y^2=z^2: \quad \text{Double cone, } z=c \implies \text{circle of radius } r = |c|; \ x=0 \implies z = \pm y; \ y=0 \implies z = \pm x
$$

$$
x^2+y^2=4: \quad \text{Cylinder, } z=c \implies \text{circle of constant radius } r = 2; \ x=0 \implies y = \pm 2; \ y=0 \implies x = \pm 2
$$

$$
\text{The cone radius scales as } |z| \text{ by homogeneity; the cylinder radius is constant because } z \text{ is a free variable}
$$

---

## Verification

**Pass 1 — Algebraic check:** Compute the normal vectors via the gradients:
- For the cone $F(x,y,z) = x^2 + y^2 - z^2$: $\nabla F = (2x, 2y, -2z)$. At $(2, 0, 2)$, $\nabla F = (4, 0, -4)$, which forms an angle of $45^\circ$ with the $z$-axis (slanted side).
- For the cylinder $G(x,y,z) = x^2 + y^2 - 4$: $\nabla G = (2x, 2y, 0)$. The $z$-component is identically zero everywhere, confirming the tangent planes are strictly vertical (parallel to $Oz$).

**Pass 2 — Numerical check:**
- On the cone, test $(0, 5, 5) \implies 0^2 + 5^2 = 5^2$ (radius is 5 at $z=5$); test $(0, 0, 0) \implies 0^2 + 0^2 = 0^2$ (radius is 0 at $z=0$).
- On the cylinder, test $(2, 0, 100) \implies 2^2 + 0^2 = 4$; test $(2, 0, -100) \implies 2^2 + 0^2 = 4$. The radius remains 2 at any height.

**Pass 3 — Consistency check:** The intersection of each surface with a sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = R^2$:
- For the cone: $z^2 + z^2 = R^2 \implies z = \pm R/\sqrt{2}$ (two horizontal circles at heights proportional to $R$).
- For the cylinder: $4 + z^2 = R^2 \implies z = \pm \sqrt{R^2 - 4}$ (exists only for $R \ge 2$).

---

## Exam tip

If an equation in 3D is missing one variable completely, it always represents a cylinder whose cross-sections parallel to the missing coordinate's axis are straight lines.
