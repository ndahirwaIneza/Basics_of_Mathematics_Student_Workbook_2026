# Exercise 11

**Problem:** The region under the graph

$$
y=\sqrt{x},\qquad x\in[0,4],
$$

is rotated about the $x$-axis.

1. Construct the integral describing the volume using the disk method.
2. Compute the volume.
3. Explain where the factor $\pi y^2$ in the integral comes from.

---

## Relevant theorem / definition

By the disk method, the volume of the solid of revolution generated by rotating the region under a non-negative continuous function $y = f(x)$ on $[a, b]$ about the $x$-axis is given by:

$$
V = \int_a^b \pi [f(x)]^2\,dx = \int_a^b \pi y^2\,dx
$$

where $A(x) = \pi [f(x)]^2$ is the area of a circular cross-section perpendicular to the rotation axis at position $x$.

---

## Solution

1. Construction of the integral using the disk method:
   Slice the solid perpendicular to the $x$-axis at an arbitrary point $x \in [0, 4]$.
   Each cross-section is a solid circular disk centered on the $x$-axis.
   The radius of the disk at position $x$ is the vertical distance from the rotation axis ($y = 0$) to the curve $y = \sqrt{x}$:

   $$
   r(x) = y = \sqrt{x}
   $$

   The area of this circular cross-section is:

   $$
   A(x) = \pi [r(x)]^2 = \pi (\sqrt{x})^2 = \pi x
   $$

   Assigning an infinitesimal thickness $dx$ to each slice produces the differential volume element:

   $$
   dV = A(x)\,dx = \pi x\,dx
   $$

   Integrating all disks along the interval $[0, 4]$ constructs the definite volume integral:

   $$
   V = \int_0^4 \pi y^2\,dx = \pi \int_0^4 x\,dx
   $$

2. Computation of the volume:
   Evaluate the definite integral using the power rule:

   $$
   \begin{align}
   V &= \pi \int_0^4 x\,dx \\
   &= \pi \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} \right]_0^4 \\
   &= \pi \left( \frac{4^2}{2} - \frac{0^2}{2} \right) \\
   &= \pi \left( \frac{16}{2} \right) \\
   &= 8\pi
   \end{align}
   $$

3. Explanation of the factor $\pi y^2$:
   When the planar region rotates about the line $y = 0$ ($x$-axis), every vertical line segment connecting $(x, 0)$ to $(x, y)$ sweeps out a flat circular disk in 3-dimensional space.
   The segment has length $y = f(x)$, which represents the radius $r$ of the resulting circle.
   According to Euclidean geometry, the area of a circle with radius $r$ is:

   $$
   \text{Area} = \pi r^2
   $$

   Substituting $r = y$ yields the cross-sectional area $\pi y^2$.
   Multiplying this area by the axial thickness $dx$ gives the volume of an approximating thin cylinder, $dV = (\pi y^2)dx$. Summing these infinitesimal cylinders across the interval gives the Riemann integral.

---

## Result

$$
V = \int_0^4 \pi (\sqrt{x})^2\,dx = \pi \int_0^4 x\,dx
$$

$$
V = 8\pi \approx 25.13
$$

$$
\pi y^2 \text{ is the area of a circular disk of radius } r = y \text{ perpendicular to the } x\text{-axis}
$$

---

## Verification

**Pass 1 — Algebraic check:** Compute the volume using the cylindrical shell method (integrating with respect to $y$). The range of $y$ is $[0, \sqrt{4}] = [0, 2]$. For a horizontal slice at height $y$, the radius of revolution is $y$ and the shell height is $4 - x = 4 - y^2$:

$$
V = 2\pi \int_0^2 y (4 - y^2)\,dy = 2\pi \int_0^2 (4y - y^3)\,dy = 2\pi \left[ 2y^2 - \frac{y^4}{4} \right]_0^2 = 2\pi (8 - 4) = 8\pi
$$

Both the disk method and the shell method give exactly $8\pi$.

**Pass 2 — Numerical check:** The solid is a circular paraboloid enclosed within a cylinder of radius $R = y(4) = 2$ and height $H = 4$.
The bounding cylinder volume is:

$$
V_{\text{cyl}} = \pi R^2 H = \pi (2^2)(4) = 16\pi
$$

By Archimedes' formula for a paraboloid of revolution, its volume is exactly half the volume of the circumscribing cylinder:

$$
V_{\text{paraboloid}} = \frac{1}{2} V_{\text{cyl}} = \frac{1}{2}(16\pi) = 8\pi
$$

**Pass 3 — Consistency check:** Dimensionally, the integrand is $[\text{length}]^2$ and the differential is $[\text{length}]$, yielding $[\text{length}]^3$, matching volume. The result is strictly positive and bounded by the cylinder volume ($0 < 8\pi < 16\pi$).

---

## Exam tip

Remember that rotating about the $x$-axis uses $\pi \int y^2\,dx$, whereas rotating about the $y$-axis uses $\pi \int x^2\,dy$ (disk method) or $2\pi \int xy\,dx$ (shell method).
