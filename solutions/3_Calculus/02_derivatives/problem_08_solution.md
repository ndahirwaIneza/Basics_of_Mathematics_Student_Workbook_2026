# Exercise 8

**Problem:** For
$$f(x)=e^{2x-3}+5^x$$
compute the derivative. Explain why the factor $2$ appears in the first term and why $\ln5$ appears in the second.

---

## Relevant theorem / definition

The chain rule applied to exponential functions gives:
$$
\frac{d}{dx}[e^{u(x)}] = e^{u(x)} \cdot u'(x)
$$
For a general exponential function with base $a > 0$, the derivative is:
$$
\frac{d}{dx}(a^x) = a^x \ln a
$$
which follows from the identity $a^x = e^{x \ln a}$ combined with the chain rule.

---

## Solution

1. Differentiate the first term $e^{2x-3}$:
   Let $u(x) = 2x - 3$. The outer function is $e^u$, and its derivative with respect to $u$ is $e^u$.
   The derivative of the inner function is:
   $$
   u'(x) = \frac{d}{dx}(2x - 3) = 2.
   $$
   By the chain rule:
   $$
   \frac{d}{dx}(e^{2x-3}) = e^{2x-3} \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(2x - 3) = 2e^{2x-3}.
   $$

2. Differentiate the second term $5^x$:
   Using the general exponential rule for base $a = 5$:
   $$
   \frac{d}{dx}(5^x) = 5^x \ln 5.
   $$

3. Combine the terms:
   $$
   f'(x) = 2e^{2x-3} + 5^x \ln 5.
   $$

4. Explain the origin of the factors:
   - **Why factor $2$ appears in the first term:** The exponent $2x - 3$ is a linear inner function whose derivative is $2$. Under the chain rule $\frac{d}{dx}[e^{u(x)}] = e^{u(x)} \cdot u'(x)$, the factor $2$ is the internal rate of change $u'(x)$.
   - **Why factor $\ln 5$ appears in the second term:** The base is $5$ rather than the natural base $e$. Expressing $5^x$ with natural base yields $5^x = e^{\ln(5^x)} = e^{x \ln 5}$. Differentiating $e^{x \ln 5}$ by the chain rule gives $e^{x \ln 5} \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(x \ln 5) = 5^x \ln 5$. The constant $\ln 5$ is the natural scaling factor arising from the change of base.

---

## Result

$$
f'(x) = 2e^{2x-3} + 5^x \ln 5
$$

$$
\text{Factor } 2\text{: generated by the chain rule as the derivative of the inner linear exponent } (2x-3)' = 2.
$$

$$
\text{Factor } \ln 5\text{: generated by the change of base } 5^x = e^{x\ln 5} \text{ whose exponent derivative is } (x\ln 5)' = \ln 5.
$$

---

## Verification

**Pass 1 — Algebraic check:** Integrate $f'(x) = 2e^{2x-3} + (\ln 5)5^x$:
$$
\int 2e^{2x-3}\,dx = 2 \cdot \frac{e^{2x-3}}{2} = e^{2x-3}
$$
$$
\int (\ln 5)5^x\,dx = (\ln 5)\frac{5^x}{\ln 5} = 5^x
$$
Summing the antiderivatives gives $e^{2x-3} + 5^x + C = f(x) + C$, confirming the derivative.

**Pass 2 — Numerical check:** Evaluate at $x = 1$:
$$
f(1) = e^{-1} + 5 \approx 0.3678794 + 5 = 5.3678794
$$
$$
f'(1) = 2e^{-1} + 5\ln 5 \approx 2(0.3678794) + 5(1.6094379) = 0.7357589 + 8.0471896 = 8.7829485
$$
Compute forward difference quotient with $h = 0.001$:
$f(1.001) = e^{2(1.001)-3} + 5^{1.001} = e^{-0.998} + 5 \cdot 5^{0.001} \approx 0.3686161 + 5(1.0016107) = 5.3766698$.
$$
\frac{f(1.001) - f(1)}{0.001} = \frac{5.3766698 - 5.3678794}{0.001} \approx 8.790 \approx 8.783 = f'(1)
$$

**Pass 3 — Consistency check:** Since $e^{2x-3} > 0$ and $5^x > 0$ for all real $x$, and both coefficients $2 > 0$ and $\ln 5 \approx 1.609 > 0$ are positive, $f'(x) > 0$ holds strictly on $\mathbb{R}$. This is consistent with both $e^{2x-3}$ and $5^x$ being strictly increasing everywhere.

---

## Exam tip

Do not apply the power rule to exponential terms: $\frac{d}{dx}(5^x) \neq x 5^{x-1}$, but rather $5^x \ln 5$.
